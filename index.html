3. Growth contribution

To estimate how industries contributed to overall growth, each sector’s prior-year share of state GDP is multiplied by its year-over-year growth rate. The resulting measure approximates the percentage points each sector contributed to state GDP growth.

\[ \text{Growth contribution}_{i,t} = \text{GDP share}_{i,t-1} \times \text{Industry growth rate}_{i,t} \]

Because 2010 has no prior-year observation, the contribution analysis covers 2011–2024.

Show code 
gdp_df = gdp_df.sort_values(by=['STATE/REGION','NAICS','YEAR']).reset_index(drop=True)

gdp_df['YoY CHANGE'] = gdp_df.groupby(['STATE/REGION','NAICS'])['GDP'].pct_change(fill_method=None) * 100
gdp_df['GDP_SHARE'] = gdp_df['GDP'] / gdp_df['OVERALL_GDP']
gdp_df['GDP_SHARE_PY'] = gdp_df.groupby(['STATE/REGION','NAICS'])['GDP_SHARE'].shift(1)
gdp_df['GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION'] = gdp_df['GDP_SHARE_PY'] * gdp_df['YoY CHANGE']

sector_contribution = (gdp_df
    .loc[(gdp_df['LEVEL'] == 2) & gdp_df['YEAR'].between(2011, 2024)]
    .groupby(['NAICS_LDESC'])['GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION']
    .sum()
    .reset_index()
    .sort_values(by='GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION', ascending=False))

These totals aggregate contributions to state economies across multiple years; they rank sectors’ relative influence but do not represent a single national GDP growth rate.

4. International trade

To place domestic growth in an international context, this section examines state import and export data from the U.S. Census Bureau. The first comparison sums trade values by industry from 2010 through 2024.

Overall export and import value

The chart below displays cumulative exports and imports on the same scale to compare both sector size and the balance between the two forms of trade.

Manufactured goods dominated both exports and imports, accounting for approximately $20.0 trillion in exports and $31.5 trillion in imports from 2010 through 2024.

How state trade exposure changed

Trade exposure measures each state’s exports or imports as a percentage of its total GDP. The KPIs below show that export exposure declined in a large majority of states. Among states where import exposure increased, the average rise was about 2 percentage points—a relatively modest shift.

5. Tennessee

Tennessee ranked among the top ten states in both total GDP growth and GDP-per-capita growth. This section identifies the sectors where Tennessee was a top-five state nationally and shows how broad sector groups contributed to its year-over-year GDP growth.

Tennessee’s growth-contribution timeline

The stacked bars show how broad sector groups combined to produce Tennessee’s annual GDP growth. Positive segments added to growth; negative segments offset it. Durable and nondurable manufacturing are excluded because they overlap the combined manufacturing-and-information group.

After 2020, finance, insurance, real estate, rental, and leasing was Tennessee’s most consistent source of growth, contributing approximately 1.4 to 1.9 percentage points annually through 2024. Arts, entertainment, recreation, accommodation, and food services also rebounded sharply from its 2020 decline. Its average contribution during 2021–2024 was roughly three times its pre-2020 average.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis state GDP data and U.S. Census Bureau state import and export data, using the prepared project files in this repository.

## 3. Growth contribution

To estimate how industries contributed to overall growth, each sector's prior-year share of state GDP is multiplied by its year-over-year growth rate. The resulting measure approximates the percentage points each sector contributed to state GDP growth.

$$
\text{Growth contribution}_{i,t}
= \text{GDP share}_{i,t-1} \times \text{Industry growth rate}_{i,t}
$$

*Because 2010 has no prior-year observation, the contribution analysis covers 2011–2024.*

```{python}
#| label: contribution-query
#| echo: true
#| fig-width: 11
#| fig-height: 7
#| fig-align: center
#| out-width: 100%

gdp_df = gdp_df.sort_values(by=['STATE/REGION','NAICS','YEAR']).reset_index(drop=True)

gdp_df['YoY CHANGE'] = gdp_df.groupby(['STATE/REGION','NAICS'])['GDP'].pct_change(fill_method=None) * 100
gdp_df['GDP_SHARE'] = gdp_df['GDP'] / gdp_df['OVERALL_GDP']
gdp_df['GDP_SHARE_PY'] = gdp_df.groupby(['STATE/REGION','NAICS'])['GDP_SHARE'].shift(1)
gdp_df['GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION'] = gdp_df['GDP_SHARE_PY'] * gdp_df['YoY CHANGE']

sector_contribution = (gdp_df
    .loc[(gdp_df['LEVEL'] == 2) & gdp_df['YEAR'].between(2011, 2024)]
    .groupby(['NAICS_LDESC'])['GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION']
    .sum()
    .reset_index()
    .sort_values(by='GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION', ascending=False))
```

```{python}

#| echo: false
#| fig-width: 11
#| fig-height: 7

#| out-width: 100%

lvl_3_secgroups = ['Durable goods manufacturing','Nondurable goods manufacturing']
tennessee_sector_groups = (gdp_df
    .loc[(gdp_df['REGION_NAME'] == 'Tennessee')
         & (gdp_df['LEVEL'] == 0)
         & (~gdp_df['NAICS_LDESC'].isin(lvl_3_secgroups))
         & gdp_df['YEAR'].between(START_YEAR + 1, END_YEAR)])

tennessee_timeline = (tennessee_sector_groups
    .pivot(index='YEAR', columns='NAICS_LDESC', values='GROWTH_CONTRIBUTION')
    .fillna(0))

ax = tennessee_timeline.plot(
    kind='bar',
    stacked=True,
    figsize=(14, 8),
    color=sns.color_palette('colorblind', n_colors=len(tennessee_timeline.columns)),
    width=0.8,
)
ax.axhline(0, color='black', linewidth=0.8)
ax.set_title(f'Tennessee annual GDP growth contribution by sector group, 2011–2024')
ax.set_xlabel('')
ax.set_ylabel('Contribution to GDP growth (percentage points)')
ax.tick_params(axis='x', rotation=0)
ax.grid(axis='x', visible=False)
ax.legend(title='Sector Group', bbox_to_anchor=(0.5, -0.20), loc='upper center', ncol=2, frameon=False)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

After 2020, finance, insurance, real estate, rental, and leasing was Tennessee's most consistent source of growth, contributing approximately 1.4 to 1.9 percentage points annually through 2024. Arts, entertainment, recreation, accommodation, and food services also rebounded sharply from its 2020 decline. Its average contribution during 2021–2024 was roughly three times its pre-2020 average.
